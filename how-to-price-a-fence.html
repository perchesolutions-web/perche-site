How to price a fence per linear foot.

A price sheet you can quote from in a minute is the difference between answering a homeowner now and calling them back tomorrow. Here is how to build one.

Book a 15-minute demoTry the demo

Start with installed price per foot, by type and height

Fence price is mostly length times a per-foot rate. The rate depends on the material and the height.

MaterialVinyl, wood, chain link and aluminum all carry different costs and lifespans.
HeightA 6 ft privacy fence costs more per foot than a 4 ft one. Price each height you sell.
Installed, not materialsQuote one number that already includes labor, posts, concrete and hardware, so the homeowner can compare.
A range, not a pointReal yards vary. A range such as $X to $Y per foot is honest and still lets the homeowner decide.

Price the extras separately

These are where quotes go wrong. List them as their own lines.

GatesWalk gates and drive gates cost far more than the fence they replace. Price each type per gate.
Old fence removalCharge per foot. Many homeowners need it and forget to ask.
CornersEach corner adds a post and labor. A small per-corner charge keeps a complex yard from underpricing.
Slope, roots and accessUneven ground, tree roots, rock and no truck access to the backyard each add time. Use a percent, a per-foot add or a flat fee.

Set a minimum job and a rounding rule

Short runs lose money on travel and setup. A minimum job protects you. Rounding quotes to a clean number, such as the nearest $50, keeps the estimate from looking falsely precise.

Put your price sheet where homeowners can use it

Once the numbers are written down, TraceQuote turns them into a page where homeowners trace their fence and see your price. You keep control of every number.

Questions

How do I price a fence per linear foot?

Decide an installed price per foot for each material and height, then add separate lines for gates, old fence removal, corners, and difficult site conditions. Add a minimum job.

Should I show a range or a single price?

A range is more honest for an estimate based on a satellite measurement. You confirm the final price after measuring on site.

Can I put my own prices in TraceQuote?

Yes. Every number in the tool comes from your price sheet and you can change it any time.
